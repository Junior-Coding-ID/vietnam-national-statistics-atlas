People

102.3 million residents on NSO's count — and a demographic turn: fertility has fallen below replacement level, the median age is 33.4, and the working-age population is ageing. Two different unemployment counts tell two different stories, and both are shown here.

V7 · The fertility inflection

Population keeps growing (bars) while the total fertility rate falls (line). The UN estimate for 2025 is 1.88 births per woman; NSO's civil registration records 1.66 — shown as a red diamond, because the two count different things.

Source: UN World Population Prospects 2024 (medium variant) for population and TFR; NSO civil registration (CRVS, 12 Nov 2026) for the 1.66 point. Only years the UN publishes are plotted — 2016–2019 are omitted rather than interpolated.

V8 · One labour market, two unemployment rates

NSO's survey counts working-age unemployment at 2.22% (urban 2.51%, rural 2.02%). The ILO-modelled rate is 1.52%. The gap is definition, not error: NSO surveys people of working age in Viet Nam; the ILO model estimates total unemployment on an internationally comparable basis. Youth unemployment (15–24) is 6.2% on the ILO model — four times the headline rate.

Source: NSO labour force survey 2025; ILO modelled estimates via World Bank WDI (SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS) and ILOSTAT, 2025. Quote the definition with the number.
